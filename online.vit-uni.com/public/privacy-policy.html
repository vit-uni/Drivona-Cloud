1. Introduction

This Privacy Policy applies to the Drivona mobile application and its accompanying hardware device (collectively, the “Service”). We understand the importance of your personal data and are committed to collecting, using, and protecting your personal information in accordance with this Privacy Policy and applicable laws and regulations, including the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, (EU) 2016/679), the UK Data Protection Act 2018, and the ePrivacy Directive.

Please read this Privacy Policy carefully before using the App. This Policy is a transparency notice under Articles 13 and 14 GDPR. It does not itself request your consent, and your use of the App does not constitute consent to any processing.

Where a processing activity is based on your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR) — for example, the optional Data Improvement Program (Section 2.4) and analytics technologies (Section 5) — we will request that consent separately, at the relevant time, through a clear affirmative action: an in-app toggle that is switched OFF by default and that you must actively switch ON. No pre-ticked boxes, bundled purposes, or consent inferred from inactivity will be used. When consent is requested, it will be presented separately from other information, in clear and plain language (Article 7(2) GDPR).

Where a processing activity is based on another legal basis — such as performance of the contract (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR) — no consent is required or requested, and refusing or withdrawing consent will never restrict your access to the core alert features of the Service. You may withdraw consent at any time, as easily as it was given, via Settings → Privacy or by contacting info@techvsystems.de (our DPO). Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before withdrawal (Article 7(3) GDPR).

2. Data We Collect

2.1 Location Data (Required for Core Functionality)

To provide speed camera alerts, road hazard warnings, and speeding alerts, this App needs to continuously collect and process your precise location data, including:

  • GPS coordinates (latitude and longitude)
  • Vehicle speed information
  • Direction of travel and route
  • Timestamps

Legal Basis: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR — necessary for the performance of a contract (providing the alert service you have subscribed to).

2.2 Community Report Data

When you actively report speed cameras, traffic accidents, road works, or other road hazards via the device button or the App, we carry out distinct processing operations, each based on a single legal basis:

(a) Receiving, validating, and abuse-prevention screening of your report (event type, coordinates, timestamp, associated Device ID) — Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of the contract), as community reporting is a core feature of the Service you request. Device IDs used for de-duplication and fraud screening are stripped at ingestion and never forwarded.

(b) Aggregating and redistributing event data to the user community — Before any event data is redistributed to other users, we apply the following technical measures to minimise re-identification risk:

(i) Identifier stripping: Device IDs and any other direct identifiers are stripped at ingestion and never forwarded;

(ii) Spatial generalisation: Coordinates are snapped to a coarse geographic grid (currently a [200‑metre / 500‑metre] hexagonal grid), obscuring precise report origin; and

(iii) Temporal rounding:Timestamps are rounded to the nearest [5‑minute] interval, reducing the granularity of any dataset that could be correlated with an individual's movements.

Even with these measures, precise location and time data — even without direct identifiers -present a residual risk of singling out an individual when correlated with other available data. Accordingly, we do not assert that the redistributed event data is irreversibly anonymised, and we treat it as pseudonymised data within the scope of the GDPR.

Legal basis. The processing and redistribution of this pseudonymised event data is based on Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interests), supported by a documented Legitimate Interests Assessment ("LIA"). The substance of the LIA is:

  • Purpose: maintaining an accurate, real-time community hazard-alert database that users of the Service reasonably expect;
  • Necessity: real‑time redistribution cannot be achieved by less intrusive means without destroying the core community-alert functionality;
  • Balance: the processing is confined to road‑event metadata, direct identifiers are removed, and spatial/temporal generalisation substantially reduces any residual re-identification risk. Users submit reports voluntarily on an effectively anonymous basis and can control their reporting activity at any time. We have concluded that
  • the interests and fundamental rights of data subjects do not override our legitimate interests in redistributing this data for the benefit of the user community.
  • You have the right to object to this processing at any time (see Section 6). A summary of the LIA may be requested from info@techvsystems.de.

2.3 Device and Account Information

  • Unique device identifier (Device ID / UUID)

2.4 Driving Behavior Data

To provide speeding alerts, we process real-time comparisons between your current speed and local speed limits. This comparison occurs in real time and is not retained.

Optional Data Improvement Program (Consent-Based, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR): You may choose to participate in a program under which anonymized driving-behavior data is retained and analyzed to improve the accuracy of our alert database. Participation requires prior, freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous consent (Article 4(11) GDPR) obtained through an explicit opt-in action. The participation toggle is OFF by default; declining has no effect on the availability or quality of the core alert features. You may withdraw consent at any time in Settings → Privacy; previously retained program data will be deleted within 30 days of withdrawal.

4. Data Sharing and Third-Party Processing

We do not sell your personal data. We share data with third parties only in the following circumstances:

4.1 Data Processors

  • Cloud infrastructure providers (e.g., AWS / Google Cloud / Azure)
  • Incident reporting and map data providers
  • Payment processing services (e.g., Apple In-App Purchase / Google Play Billing)

4.2 Legal Requirements

We may be required to disclose necessary data upon receipt of legally binding court orders or governmental requests.

4.3 Anonymized Community Event Data

Road event reports you submit (event type, coordinates, and timestamp) are made available to the entire user community to power the real‑time hazard‑alert service. Before redistribution, each report undergoes de-identification and generalisation processing as described in Section

2.2(b):

Direct identifiers (Device ID / UUID) are stripped at ingestion and never forwarded;

Coordinates are snapped to a coarse geographic grid; and

Timestamps are rounded to the nearest [5‑minute] interval.

Because precise spatial and temporal data carries a residual risk of singling out an individual — even after these measures — the redistributed dataset is treated as pseudonymised data within the meaning of the GDPR, rather than as irreversibly anonymised data falling outside its scope. It is therefore subject to the safeguards and legal bases set out in this Policy.

The redistribution of this pseudonymised event data is carried out on the basis of legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR), as detailed in Section 2.2(b). You have the right to object to this processing at any time (see Section 6). We do not sell or license this data to any third party for commercial purposes, and we do not use it for advertising, profiling, or automated decision-making.

4.4 International Data Transfers

Some of the processors listed in Section 4.1 operate infrastructure outside the EEA. Transfers currently occur to the following third countries:

  • United States — cloud infrastructure (alert backend, storage), push notification delivery, crash reporting
  • United Kingdom — Transfers of personal data to the United Kingdom aremade on the basis of the European Commission's adequacy decision of28 June 2021 (as extended), recognising the UK as providing an adequatelevel of protection. For transfers from the UK to other third countries,we rely on the UK International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA) or theUK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, as applicable.
  • No other third countries are currently used for the processing described in this Policy beyond the United States entry above.

Transfer mechanism. Transfers to countries without an adequacy decision rely on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914 of 4 June 2021 — Module One (controller-to-processor) for the processors in Section 4.1, and Module Two (controller-to-controller) where applicable — as incorporated into our data processing agreements. Where a recipient is certified under the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework, we additionally rely on the Framework’s adequacy decision with respect to that recipient.

Transfer Impact Assessment. Before authorizing each transfer, we conduct and document a Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) in line with EDPB Recommendations 01/2020. Each TIA: (i) identifies the destination country and the relevant third-country legislation applicable to the recipient (including, for U.S. recipients, FISA §702 and E.O. 12333); (ii) assesses whether such law could require disclosure of the transferred data in a manner incompatible with GDPR; and (iii) determines supplementary measures where needed. TIAs are reviewed annually and upon material changes in the legal environment.

Supplementary measures. We apply data minimization (no content data is transferred; identifiers are pseudonymized where technically feasible), encryption in transit and at rest, and contractual commitments requiring the recipient to notify us of any government access request to the extent legally permitted and to challenge such requests.

Your right to a copy. You may request a copy of the applicable SCCs (or the parts relevant to you) free of charge at any time by contacting info@techvsystems.de, in accordance with Art. 13(1)(f) GDPR.

We will update this Section whenever a new transfer destination or mechanism is introduced and notify you of material changes per Section 10.

5. Cookies, SDKs, and Technologies on Your Device

This App stores and accesses information on your terminal equipment (cookies and equivalent technologies, including SDK identifiers) within the meaning of Article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive as implemented in the national law of applicable EU/EEA Member States.

5.1 Strictly Necessary Technologies (No Consent Required)

Used only where strictly necessary to provide the Service you request or to secure and stabilize it:

  • Device pairing configuration and alert preferences
  • Real-time location handling required to deliver alerts
  • Security, fraud prevention, and load balancing
  • Storage of your prior consent choices (to avoid repeated prompts)

These cannot be switched off because the Service cannot function without them.

5.2 Non-Essential Technologies (Prior Opt-In Consent Required)

All other technologies — including analytics SDKs and usage measurement — are disabled by default and remain inactive until you have given prior consent through an explicit affirmative action. We do not use advertising cookies, cross-app tracking, or device fingerprinting for tracking purposes.

5.3 Consent Mechanics

  • Granularity: Consent is requested per purpose (analytics is separate from the Data Improvement Program); purposes are never bundled.
  • Equal ease: Accepting and refusing require the same number of steps and equally prominent interfaces. No dark patterns, nagging, or re-prompting after refusal.
  • No implied consent: Continued use of the App, scrolling, or dismissing the notice does not constitute consent.
  • Records: For each consent, we log when, how, and for which purposes it was given, so we can demonstrate compliance (Article 7(1) GDPR).
  • Withdrawal: Via Settings → Privacy → Consent, as easily as consent was given (Article 7(3) GDPR).

5.4 Platform Frameworks

Apple’s App Tracking Transparency (ATT) framework is not engaged, because the App does not track you across other companies’ apps or websites for advertising or measurement, and does not share data with data brokers.

6. Your Rights (GDPR / UK GDPR)

Under the GDPR and UK data protection law, you have the following rights:

  • Right of Access — Request a copy of the personal data we hold about you
  • Right to Rectification — Request correction of inaccurate personal data
  • Right to Erasure (“Right to be Forgotten”) — Request deletion of your personal data
  • Right to Restrict Processing — Request restriction of processing of your data
  • Right to Data Portability — Obtain your data in a structured, commonly used format and transfer it to another service
  • Right to Object — Object to data processing based on legitimate interests
  • Right to Withdraw Consent — Withdraw consent for consent-based data processing at any time

How to Exercise Your Rights: Send an email to kontakt@techvsystems.de or info@techvsystems.de (our DPO). We will respond within one month of receipt of your request. Where necessary, taking into account the complexity and number of requests, this period may be extended by a further two months; if so, we will inform you of the extension and the reasons for it within one month of receipt of the original request (Art. 12(3) GDPR).

Right to Lodge a Complaint: You have the right to lodge a complaint with your local data protection supervisory authority (e.g., the Danish Datatilsynet, the Irish DPC, the UK ICO, the German BfDI, etc.).

Consent is used as a legal basis only for the optional processing described in Sections 2.4 and 5.2. The core alert features do not depend on consent; therefore, refusing or withdrawing consent never limits those features. Requests to exercise any of the above rights are free of charge.
